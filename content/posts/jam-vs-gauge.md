**Jam tracks six AI engines on every plan and starts at $299 a month for 400 prompts. Gauge Growth costs $599 a month for 600 prompts on three engines.** Both run competitor gap analysis and write articles. Pick Jam if you want wider engine coverage per dollar, articles shipped as pull requests, and cold email outreach in the same tool. Pick Gauge if you want a fixed 54,000-answer daily tracking plan, direct publishing to Webflow, Framer and Sanity, and an Action Center for assigning work.

## What does each tool track?

Jam tracks [ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, Google AI Overviews and Microsoft Copilot](https://www.spreadjam.com/pricing). All six are in every plan. You choose between 50 and 1,000 tracked prompts and a scan cadence of weekly, every other day or daily. Jam reports a [Visibility Score, the share of tracked prompts where you are cited](https://www.spreadjam.com/).

Gauge Chat is Gauge's AI visibility product. Its [Growth plan runs 600 prompts daily on ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and Microsoft Copilot](https://www.withgauge.com/pricing). Google AI Overviews, Gemini and Perplexity are core platforms only on Enterprise. Claude and Grok are optional on both plans if you bring your own API keys.

Gauge's own copy is not consistent on this. Its [AI visibility page lists ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot, AI Mode and AI Overviews](https://www.withgauge.com/ai-visibility), while the pricing page limits Growth to three core platforms. Treat the pricing page as the plan you actually buy.

Gauge reports two metrics, [citation rate and mention rate, tracked daily](https://www.withgauge.com/ai-visibility). Gauge also names [Reddit and YouTube citation insights and ChatGPT Ads tracking](https://www.withgauge.com/) among its features. Jam does not list those.

## How do Jam and Gauge price prompts and answers?

Gauge defines an answer as [one prompt on one platform on one day](https://www.withgauge.com/gauge-vs-otterly-ai). Growth is 600 prompts x 3 platforms x 30 days, which gives [54,000 answers a month for $599](https://www.withgauge.com/pricing). Gauge puts that at [$11.09 per 1,000 answers](https://www.withgauge.com/gauge-vs-otterly-ai).

Jam's default plan is [400 prompts across 6 platforms, scanned every other day, for $299](https://www.spreadjam.com/pricing). On the same definition that is 400 x 6 x 15 scans, or 36,000 answers a month. $299 for 36,000 answers works out below Gauge's $11.09 per 1,000. Scan cadence and credits are adjustable in Jam's advanced pricing settings, so the figure moves if you scan daily.

Gauge tracks more answers in total, 54,000 against 36,000. Jam's cost per answer is lower, and it covers six engines instead of three.

Two pricing details to know:

- Jam charges for work in credits. [An article costs about 35 credits, shipping it as a pull request costs 20 more, and a deep research report costs about 50](https://www.spreadjam.com/pricing). The default plan includes 2,000 credits a month, which covers about 36 articles shipped as pull requests. [Credit top-ups never expire.](https://www.spreadjam.com/pricing) Jam shows its price for the 400-prompt plan as text and sells anything above 1,000 prompts as Enterprise.
- Gauge Growth bundles [18 articles a month and 3 seats](https://www.withgauge.com/pricing). Gauge does not publish Enterprise pricing; it is [custom and sold through a demo](https://www.withgauge.com/pricing). Sentiment analysis is [an Enterprise feature](https://www.withgauge.com/gauge-vs-otterly-ai).

## Which one is better for competitor gap analysis?

Both find the prompts where competitors are named and you are not.

Gauge's gap analysis shows [specific prompts where competitors get mentioned but you do not, plus the content formats that perform best on each AI platform](https://www.withgauge.com/ai-visibility). Its citation view gives [domain-level and URL-level data filtered by topic, model and date](https://www.withgauge.com/ai-visibility). Recommendations land in an Action Center where you can [review, assign and track them](https://www.withgauge.com/).

Jam's gap analysis is [included in every plan](https://www.spreadjam.com/pricing) and is built to show [where you are not cited across AI platforms](https://www.spreadjam.com/). It sits next to Search Console data, keyword and SERP analysis and a content plan, so a gap turns into a planned article inside the same tool.

If your team needs to assign gaps to named owners and track them, Gauge has the more developed workflow tooling. If a single marketer owns the whole loop, Jam covers it with fewer moving parts.

## How do they handle content?

Gauge's content engine [generates briefs, outlines and articles, then publishes them](https://www.withgauge.com/). Growth includes 18 articles a month and publishes to [Webflow, Framer, Sanity and GitHub](https://www.withgauge.com/pricing).

Jam researches and writes articles and [ships them as pull requests](https://www.spreadjam.com/pricing). That fits teams whose blog lives in a repository. Jam lists no direct Webflow, Framer or Sanity publishing, so a team on one of those CMSs would paste the content in by hand.

## What else does each product do?

Jam also runs outreach. Its homepage shows [a shared inbox, lead enrichment, automatic mailbox warm-up and custom automations](https://www.spreadjam.com/), with approvals through Slack and GitHub. Teams that want AI visibility and prospecting in one place get both from Jam.

Gauge splits into two products. Gauge Chat covers AI search visibility. [Gauge Agents measures how coding agents choose and use a developer product](https://www.withgauge.com/), with hosted plans from $100 to $1,000 a month and a free plan with no card. Gauge says agent preference and agent experience currently focus on developer tools. An open-source CLI runs those evaluations locally. If you sell to developers and care about coding-agent adoption, Gauge covers ground Jam does not.

## Who should pick Jam, and who should pick Gauge?

**Pick Jam if:**

- You want all six major engines, including Gemini, Perplexity and AI Overviews, without moving to an Enterprise plan.
- You want a lower entry price and a lower cost per tracked answer.
- Your content ships through GitHub pull requests.
- You also run cold outbound and want it in the same tool.
- You want to test first: Jam offers a [14-day trial](https://www.spreadjam.com/pricing).

**Pick Gauge if:**

- You want a fixed plan with 600 prompts tracked daily and 54,000 answers a month.
- You publish to Webflow, Framer or Sanity and want direct publishing.
- You need an Action Center to assign work, or Reddit, YouTube and ChatGPT Ads tracking.
- You sell to developers and want coding-agent evaluation through Gauge Agents.
- You want to buy without a sales call. Gauge says [Growth needs none](https://www.withgauge.com/gauge-vs-otterly-ai).

Gauge frames Growth for [challenger brands and Enterprise for leading brands](https://www.withgauge.com/pricing). Jam is built for growth teams and founders who want AI engines to cite them when prospects look for product recommendations.

## Where does each fall short?

Jam's gaps:

- Its [pricing page](https://www.spreadjam.com/pricing) shows a price as text for the 400-prompt plan only.
- Its tracked engines are the [six listed](https://www.spreadjam.com/pricing), with no Claude or Grok.
- Sentiment analysis is not among its [included features](https://www.spreadjam.com/pricing).
- Direct CMS publishing stops at GitHub pull requests.

Gauge's gaps:

- [Growth covers three engines](https://www.withgauge.com/pricing). Gemini, Perplexity and AI Overviews need Enterprise.
- The [entry price is $599 a month](https://www.withgauge.com/pricing), roughly double Jam's default plan.
- [Growth is capped at 3 seats and a single location](https://www.withgauge.com/pricing). A competitor review lists [a $99 Starter plan and 10 seats on Growth](https://cintra.run/blog/gauge-review), which Gauge's own pricing page does not show. Confirm current plans before you buy.
- No outreach or inbox tooling.

## FAQ

**Is Gauge cheaper than Jam?**
No. Gauge Growth is [$599 a month](https://www.withgauge.com/pricing). Jam's default plan is [$299 a month](https://www.spreadjam.com/pricing). Gauge tracks more answers in total, but its cost is [$11.09 per 1,000 answers](https://www.withgauge.com/gauge-vs-otterly-ai) and Jam's default plan comes in lower on the same definition.

**Does Gauge track Gemini and Perplexity?**
On Enterprise, yes. [Growth covers ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and Microsoft Copilot](https://www.withgauge.com/pricing). Jam tracks [Gemini and Perplexity on every plan](https://www.spreadjam.com/pricing).

**Do both write content?**
Yes. Gauge Growth includes [18 articles a month](https://www.withgauge.com/pricing). Jam's default 2,000 credits [cover about 36 articles shipped as pull requests](https://www.spreadjam.com/pricing).

**Which is better for a content marketer?**
Both fit. Jam gives you more engines and articles per dollar. Gauge gives you more CMS publishing options and an Action Center for assigning work.

**Is there a free trial?**
Jam has a [14-day trial that needs a card](https://www.spreadjam.com/pricing). Gauge's [free plan covers Gauge Agents](https://www.withgauge.com/pricing), not Gauge Chat.

**Does either replace my SEO tools?**
Neither is a full SEO suite. Jam includes [keyword, SERP and traffic analysis](https://www.spreadjam.com/pricing). Gauge connects [GA4, Search Console and DataForSEO](https://www.withgauge.com/pricing).
